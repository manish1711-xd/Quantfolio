1 · What the system does

QuantFolio turns a list of holdings — typed in, or imported unmodified from a broker CSV — into a full analytical picture of a portfolio: what it is worth, what return it earned, how much risk it carries, where that risk comes from, and what would happen to it under stress.

It is organised as 16 pages across four areas:

AreaPagesWhat they answer
PortfolioBuild · Overview · Holdings · Performance · Trades & lots What do I own, what is it worth, what did it actually return, and what are my realised gains?
RiskRisk analytics · Benchmark · Correlation & factors · Stress testing How much can I lose, how do I compare to an index, what drives my returns, and what happens in a crash?
StrategyOptimiser · Monte Carlo · Rebalance What mix would have been better, what range of outcomes is plausible, and what trades get me there?
ResearchSecurity deep dive · Watchlist · News & calendar · Export How healthy is this company, what am I tracking, what is happening today, and how do I share it?

The features that distinguish it

  • Search by company name across the whole Indian market — type Reliance or HDFC Bank, no need to know that the symbol is RELIANCE.NS. Typos are tolerated. Runs offline.
  • Broker CSV import that works on a Zerodha, Groww or Upstox export unmodified — column names are recognised automatically, and every rejected row is reported rather than silently dropped.
  • Scheduled email tearsheets — daily, weekly or monthly, delivered by a command your operating system schedules.
  • Watchlist price alerts that e-mail you once when a level is reached, not once per check.
  • News & economic calendar — live headlines matched to your holdings, plus the week's high-impact releases.
  • Fundamental quality scores computed from a company's own financial statements.

2 · The finance knowledge used

This is the substance of the product. Every calculation below is implemented from its original definition, with the convention stated in the code and in the interface.

2.1 Return and performance measurement

ConceptWhat it measures and why it is here
Time-weighted return (TWR)Return of the strategy, stripping out the effect of deposits and withdrawals. The correct basis for comparing against an index.
Money-weighted return (XIRR)Return you actually earned, including the timing of your contributions. Solved numerically and verified by checking that the net present value of the cash flows is zero at the answer.
Modified DietzA cash-flow-weighted approximation for periods without daily valuations.
FIFO tax lotsEvery buy is a lot; every sell consumes the oldest lots first. This produces realised profit and loss, holding periods and an accurate cost basis.
Geometric annualisationCompounding, not averaging — 252 trading days a year.

2.2 Risk measurement

ConceptWhat it measures and why it is here
Volatility & downside deviationTotal variability, and variability of losses only.
Value at Risk (Cornish–Fisher)Loss threshold at a confidence level, adjusted for skew and fat tails. Real returns are not normally distributed and a normal VaR understates the danger.
Conditional VaRThe average loss given that the VaR threshold was breached — what the bad days actually cost.
Kupiec backtestA statistical test of whether the VaR model was right, by counting how often it was breached. Rare in retail tools; it is what separates a risk number from a risk model.
Maximum drawdown & episodesWorst peak-to-trough fall, plus every episode with its depth, length and recovery time.
Sharpe · Sortino · Calmar · Omega · UlcerFive risk-adjusted return ratios, each penalising a different definition of risk.
Beta, alpha, tracking error, information ratioMarket sensitivity, skill after adjusting for it, and consistency of the difference.

2.3 Attribution — where risk and return come from

ConceptWhat it measures and why it is here
Marginal & component risk contributionHow much of total portfolio volatility each holding is responsible for — which is not the same as its weight. A 5% position can carry 20% of the risk.
Herfindahl index & effective number of betsConcentration. Twenty holdings can behave like four if they move together.
Principal component analysisHow many independent sources of risk actually exist in the portfolio.
Hierarchical clusteringGroups holdings by how they co-move, revealing hidden concentration that sector labels miss.
Fama–French factor regressionDecomposes returns into market, size, value, profitability and momentum exposures — with Newey–West standard errors, because return data is autocorrelated and ordinary errors would overstate significance.

2.4 Portfolio construction

ConceptWhat it measures and why it is here
Mean–variance optimisationMaximum-Sharpe and minimum-variance portfolios, and the efficient frontier, solved by sequential quadratic programming.
Covariance shrinkageA sample covariance matrix estimated from limited history is unstable; shrinkage pulls it toward a structured target so the optimiser does not act on noise.
Risk parityWeights that equalise risk contribution rather than capital.
Black–LittermanStarts from the market's own implied returns (reverse optimisation) and blends in your views with a confidence level. Solves the classic problem that raw mean–variance produces absurd, unstable weights.
Monte Carlo simulationThousands of forward paths to show a distribution of outcomes instead of a single projection.
Rebalancing trade listsThe actual buys and sells to reach a target, with drift tolerance bands.

2.5 Indian-market specifics

ConceptWhat it measures and why it is here
STCG / LTCG capital gainsIndian equity tax under Sections 111A and 112A, including the 23 July 2024 regime change (15%→20% short term, 10%→12.5% long term, exemption ₹1L→₹1.25L).
Loss set-off & carry-forwardSections 70/71/74 — set-off is mandatory in quantum and only elective in ordering, and losses carry forward eight years.
Section 112A grandfatheringThe 31 January 2018 cost-basis rule.
Tax-loss harvestingWhich lots to realise, and the tax actually saved — which is often zero, and the tool says so.
Exchange conventions.NS / .BO suffixes, NSE series (EQ/BE/BZ), BSE groups, ISIN (ISO 6166) with check-digit validation.

2.6 Derivatives and company analysis

ConceptWhat it measures and why it is here
Black–Scholes–MertonEuropean option pricing with all five Greeks, and implied volatility solved by root-finding. NSE options are European, so the model is exactly right for this market.
Covered calls, protective puts, collarsPayoff, breakeven, income, and the upside surrendered — always plotted against simply holding the shares.
Piotroski F-ScoreNine-signal test of fundamental strength (Piotroski, 2000).
Altman Z-ScoreDistress risk, using the Z″ variant and the emerging-market adjustment where appropriate (Altman 1968, 2005).
Beneish M-ScoreEight-variable screen for aggressive accounting (Beneish, 1999).
Sloan accruals ratioHow much of reported profit is not backed by cash (Sloan, 1996).
Technical indicatorsWilder RSI, MACD, Bollinger bands, 52-week positioning.
A principle that runs through all of it. Where a number cannot be computed honestly, the system says so rather than substituting a plausible value. A missing 52-week range shows as a dash, not 50%. An interest-cover ratio for a company reporting no interest expense is unavailable, not infinite. A Piotroski score built from six of nine available signals is reported as “six of eight passed”, never as a low nine — because counting a missing signal as a failure makes a healthy company look distressed.

3.2 Software architecture

The codebase follows two rules, enforced consistently:

1. Computation never imports the user interface. The interface never computes.
21 analytics modules contain zero Streamlit imports; the UI contains zero formulas. This is why ~20,000 lines of financial mathematics can be tested in seconds, offline, against hand-computed values — with no browser and no market-data subscription.
2. Every network call lives in one package.
Three modules in src/data/ touch the wire — Yahoo prices, the exchange files, the news feeds — and nothing outside it does. Replacing the price provider with a broker feed (Zerodha Kite, Upstox, Interactive Brokers) means rewriting one file against the same signatures: no change to the analytics, the interface, or the tests.

3.3 Engineering techniques demonstrated

  • Test-driven correctness through invariants. Rather than only checking fixed values, the suite asserts mathematical identities that cannot rot: beta of a series against itself is exactly 1.0; component risk contributions sum to portfolio volatility; CVaR ≤ VaR at every confidence level; put–call parity holds to 1×10-12; every option Greek matches its numerical derivative.
  • Cross-module agreement. Two independent implementations must produce the same answer — a one-factor regression beta equals the covariance/variance beta to 1×10-12.
  • Headless UI testing. All 16 pages are rendered and asserted in CI with a stubbed data layer — no browser, no network.
  • Typed interfaces. Frozen dataclasses throughout; static type checking in CI.
  • Defensive data handling. Column-name aliasing, encoding detection, timezone normalisation, schema contracts on every download, and atomic file writes with backups.
  • Caching strategy. Tiered time-to-live by data type — 5 minutes for quotes, 30 minutes for history, 24 hours for fundamentals.
  • Command-line tooling. Scheduled reports and the universe rebuild run headlessly under cron or Windows Task Scheduler, sharing the exact same computation path as the interface.

3.4 Data engineering

The instrument universe is generated, not hand-maintained. A build step pulls four official exchange files and merges them:

SourceRowsSupplies
NSE equity listing2,567Symbol, company name, series, ISIN
BSE scrip master4,991Trading symbol, issuer name, ISIN, industry
NSE bhavcopy3,633Daily turnover — used to rank search results
BSE bhavcopy5,002Daily turnover

Turnover is not decoration. Without it a full-market search ranks alphabetically: typing hdfc returned HDFC AMC ahead of HDFC Bank, and the typo relaince returned Reliance Communications (₹0.3 crore traded) ahead of Reliance Industries (₹1,767 crore). Ranking by traded value fixed both. The build also refuses to keep a symbol that two independent sources agree has stopped trading — it caught four, including TATAMOTORS.NS after the group's demerger.

4 · What it deliberately does not do

Stated plainly, because a tool's credibility rests on its limits being known rather than discovered.

LimitationWhy, and what it would take to remove
Prices are delayed, not live Yahoo Finance runs roughly 15 minutes behind for NSE/BSE. Every price carries a timestamp and a freshness label; the application never claims to be real-time. Removing it: a broker feed such as Zerodha Kite or Upstox, behind the existing data interface.
No long-horizon return forecast Predicting a 5-, 10- or 11-year return requires pairs of (accounts at year T, return from T to T+N). The data provider supplies five annual periods, which yields four one-year pairs and zero from five years out. Rather than print a number fitted to no observations, the application lists every horizon with its training-pair count and the reason. Removing it: a point-in-time fundamentals database with 15+ years of history retaining delisted companies (Compustat, CMIE Prowess, Refinitiv). This is a paid-data problem, not a modelling one.
No option chain for NSE/BSE Measured, not assumed: Yahoo returns 21 expiries for AAPL and zero for RELIANCE.NS. Options analytics therefore work from a strike and premium typed off your broker screen. Removing it: an NSE data subscription.
The app cannot schedule itself Streamlit runs only while a browser is connected. Scheduled e-mails are delivered by a command run by cron or Windows Task Scheduler — and the interface says so rather than showing a toggle that does nothing.
Headline tone is not a prediction A small documented lexicon reads the words, and always shows which ones matched. It does not know what the market had already priced in.
Insights are rules, not advice Every insight comes from a documented threshold. The application is analytical and educational; it does not make recommendations.
Why this list is an asset, not a weakness. Most retail analytics tools fail silently — a missing value becomes a zero, a stale feed keeps rendering, a model extrapolates past its data. This system was built so that each of those is caught and named. One live example: the news feeds originally specified still return valid data with a 200 OK response, but have not published since April 2024. The application measures the age of every feed and withholds any source older than seven days — so it shows nothing rather than presenting 870-day-old headlines as today's market news.

5 · Where to take it next

Ordered by value relative to effort. quick win is days of work, medium is a week or two, strategic is a larger commitment or a paid data source.

5.1 Highest value for a personal investor

FeatureEffortWhy it matters
Sector data for NSE listingsquick win BSE publishes an industry label and NSE does not, so ~2,300 NSE-only instruments currently have a blank sector. This silently weakens sector attribution and concentration analysis — the two views most people look at first.
News impact event studyquick win Measure the realised abnormal return around a stock's past news and results dates. This is the defensible answer to “what does news do to this stock”, and the data pipeline for it already exists.
Capital-gains statement exportquick win The FIFO lot engine and the Indian tax rules are already built. Producing a ready-to-file schedule of realised gains turns a feature into an annual reason to open the app.
Dividend and corporate-action trackingmedium Splits and bonuses currently rely on adjusted prices. Explicit handling gives accurate income yield and a correct cost basis.
Goal-based planningmedium “Will this portfolio fund a ₹2 crore goal in 12 years?” The Monte Carlo engine already produces the distribution; this frames it as a probability of success.
Mutual fund & SIP supportstrategic Most Indian retail wealth is in mutual funds, not direct equity. AMFI publishes daily NAVs free. This is the single largest expansion of the addressable audience.

5.2 Highest value for a company or an advisory practice

FeatureEffortWhy it matters
Multi-client / multi-account viewmedium The store already holds multiple named portfolios. Roll-ups across clients, with per-client reports, turn a personal tool into an advisory product.
Branded PDF client reportingquick win The HTML tearsheet and the scheduled e-mail already exist. Adding a logo, a cover page and an adviser footer makes it a client deliverable.
User accounts & access controlmedium Required before any multi-user deployment. Streamlit supports authentication; the data layer would move from SQLite to PostgreSQL.
Audit trailmedium Who changed what, and when. Necessary for any regulated context, and straightforward given the existing store.
Broker API integrationstrategic Zerodha Kite or Upstox would remove manual import entirely and provide real-time prices. The architecture already isolates this to one module.
Benchmark & peer comparisonmedium Compare a client portfolio against model portfolios or a peer group, not only an index.
Hosted deploymentstrategic The Docker image exists. A hosted instance with scheduled reports makes it a service rather than a program.

5.3 Analytical depth, if differentiation is the goal

  • Point-in-time fundamentals — the one purchase that would unlock genuine multi-year return modelling, cleanly and honestly.
  • Custom factor construction — build and backtest factors on the full NSE/BSE universe; the regression machinery and the instrument data are already in place.
  • Transaction-cost and slippage modelling — rebalancing currently ignores brokerage, STT and impact cost, which materially changes whether a trade is worth making.
  • Fixed income and gold — most Indian portfolios are multi-asset; equity-only analysis tells a partial story.
  • Scenario builder — user-defined shocks (“rupee falls 10%, oil rises 30%”) on top of the existing historical stress replay.
If only three things are done next: ① NSE sector data, because it silently degrades analysis people already rely on; ② the capital-gains statement, because it creates a recurring reason to use the product; ③ mutual fund support, because it is where most Indian household wealth actually sits.
